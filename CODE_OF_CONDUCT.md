# Code of conduct

SpareLine exists to keep people talking when things go wrong. We'd like working on it to feel the same: calm, respectful and useful.

## Our pledge

We want participation in this project to be welcoming and free of harassment for everyone. That includes people of every age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, and sexual identity and orientation.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good intent. Most disagreements are misunderstandings.
- Critique ideas and code, not people. Explain the why behind a review comment.
- Welcome newcomers, including people new to Rust, cryptography or accessibility.
- Accept feedback gracefully, and admit and learn from mistakes.
- Keep discussions focused on what's best for the people who rely on SpareLine.

## What we won't accept

- Harassment, insults, or derogatory or discriminatory comments.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Personal or political attacks, trolling, or deliberately derailing discussions.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and chat. It also applies when you represent the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, contact the maintainers privately at **[conduct contact email]**. Every report is reviewed and handled confidentially. Reporters won't face retaliation.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take whatever action they find appropriate, depending on what happened:

1. **Correction:** a private, written explanation of what was unacceptable, and a request to change.
2. **Warning:** a warning, with consequences for continued behaviour, and a period of no interaction with the people involved.
3. **Temporary ban:** no participation for a set period.
4. **Permanent ban:** removal from the project.

Maintainers who don't follow or enforce this code in good faith may face action from other maintainers.
